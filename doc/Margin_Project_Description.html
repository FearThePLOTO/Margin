1. High-Level Summary

Margin is a 2D pixel-art platformer, made in Godot, that teaches children aged 7 to 12 how astronauts survive. The player is Max, a young astronaut who lands on Mars. A solar radiation storm forces him into a lava tube cave with no breathable air.

To get through, players share a small suit battery between Air, Shield and Light. Every cell given to one system is taken from another, so each choice has a cost. Power stations add new cells and forecast the area ahead, so kids plan the way real mission planners balance power, oxygen and radiation protection.

The danger comes from real NASA data. At startup, the game checks NASA's DONKI database for recent solar particle storms. If none happened, it uses a real stored storm and says so. Hidden scrolls unlock short, kid-friendly science chapters with their NASA sources, such as Curiosity's radiation readings and astronaut oxygen needs.

This matters because space science often feels distant to children. Margin turns it into decisions they make with their own hands, where failing is gentle and trying a new plan is part of the fun.

2. Project Details

Main use

A child plays one complete mission, Level 1 "The Cave", and learns by doing: resources in space are limited, every choice has a cost, and radiation, air and visibility are survival problems you plan for ahead of time. The game can be played alone or used in a classroom or club, where students compare the power plans they chose and how those plans turned out.

How it works

The power trade-off (core mechanic). The suit starts with 3 battery cells. At each Power Station the player gets one new cell, picks which system receives it (up to 3 per system), then can move all cells around.

SystemMore cellsFewer cells
Air (CO2 scrubber)Oxygen drains slower (0.55 times as fast with 3 cells)Oxygen drains faster (1.45 times as fast with 0 cells)
ShieldRadiation meter fills slower (0.35 times)Radiation meter fills faster (1.6 times)
Light (headlamp)Bigger, brighter light: lava, spikes and secrets are visibleThe cave is close to black

Each station shows a forecast of the next area (lava and air drain, a radiation skylight, a dark spike tunnel), so players plan ahead instead of reacting.

Real space weather drives the danger. At startup the game asks NASA's DONKI database for Solar Energetic Particle (SEP) events from the last 30 days. A GOES-measured storm above 100 MeV is "strong" (radiation builds 1.7 times faster); above 10 MeV is "medium" (1.3 times). If there was no storm, or the device is offline, the game uses one of 16 real GOES-measured SEP events (May 2024 to November 2025) and says so. Stored data is never presented as live. The storm level and date appear in the story cutscene, on the HUD, at Power Stations and on the results screen.

Learning through the Book of Books. Level 1 hides 3 scrolls: one easy to find, one in a secret room that needs fast moves under radiation, and one tucked into a room. Each unlocks a chapter with its source: The Red Planet, Solar Storms and Living in Space. The book also records deaths and scrolls per level and is saved between sessions.

Kid-friendly design. Three resources named in plain words; short, funny dialogue; a NASA mission-control terminal that briefs Max; an annoying old wizard, OG, who laughs at the player. Failure is gentle: Max respawns at the start of the room with at least half a tank of air. A different real storm on each run makes it worth replaying.

Benefits and intended impact

  • Children feel the trade-off between air, radiation and light instead of reading about it.
  • Real storms change between runs, so the same plan can work once and fail the next time: conditions must be checked, not assumed.
  • Every fact cites its source, and an in-game NASA Data screen separates real science from game invention.
  • Our aim: children understand that astronaut survival is about planning and trade-offs, and become curious about real space science and NASA data.

Features, limitations and future plans

WORKING Delivered and playable in the project:

  • Main menu (start new game, continue from last checkpoint, options, exit) over an animated Martian dust storm; Level 1; results screen.
  • Level 1: landing cutscene and movement tutorial, the OG story cutscene, and 7 cave rooms with falling platforms, lava, a vertical climb, a risky oxygen tank, a radiation skylight, a secret room and a dark tunnel.
  • 3 Power Stations, each adding a new cell before the player re-plans.
  • Live DONKI SEP query with fallback to stored real events, source shown on screen.
  • Book of Books (3 chapters), saved between sessions.
  • Pause menu (resume, retry checkpoint, restart level, book, options, main menu); options for music, sound and fullscreen, saved; randomised music playlists.
  • Exported builds for Windows, Linux and web browser.

Where to look in the source: Level 1 in src/core/game/level_01/; data logic in src/core/autoload/space_weather.gd; power values in src/core/constants.gd; book content in src/core/autoload/book_of_books.gd.

LIMITATIONS

  • Only Level 1 is playable; Level 2 appears in the book as "Coming soon".
  • Game values are tuned for fun, not true to scale. Every effect points the same way as in real life, and the game says so.
  • Hot lava is fiction: real Martian lava tubes are cold and ancient. The NASA Data screen states this.
  • The live query uses NASA's shared DEMO_KEY, which has hourly and daily request limits; when a limit is hit or the device is offline, the game uses stored real events.
  • Only SEP events change gameplay; other sources are used in the educational text, not loaded live. Storm strength is grouped into two levels, not a full radiation model.
  • Keyboard play on desktop or browser (mouse works in menus). English only.

FUTURE PLANS Not built yet:

  • More levels, each focused on a different survival system.
  • More live NASA data, such as solar flares and CMEs from DONKI.
  • Controller and touch support; Arabic translation.
  • A teacher view that compares students' power plans.

Tools, languages, hardware and software

  • Game engine: Godot 4.7, programmed in GDScript.
  • Art and sound: our own Python 3 scripts (Pillow, NumPy) that generate the pixel art, tilesets, characters, portraits, logo and sound effects (written with SI assistance, see Section 5).
  • Data: NASA DONKI API (api.nasa.gov), NASA CCMC.
  • Fonts: Silkscreen and Pixelify Sans (SIL Open Font License).
  • Version control: Git and GitHub. Hardware: developed and played on desktop PCs.

Creativity and design factors

Margin mixes a tight, fast platformer with a strategy layer about resource planning. Live space weather means real events on the Sun change the game. Humour comes from OG, a rude old wizard who laughs at Max, and collectible scrolls reward exploration with real science instead of points. We designed for ages 7 to 12 (short dialogue, big readable pixel text, three plain-word resources), honesty (stored data never shown as live, sources cited, fiction labelled), reliability (works offline or when the API limit is hit), replayability, and no NASA logos or mission insignia in the artwork.
